## Title

WikiMed Article Recommender: An Open-Source Dashboard for Data-Driven Prioritization of Cancer-Related Wikipedia Article Editing

Zach Schneider-Lynch, Ed.M.; Jeremy Warner, MD, MS, FAMIA, FASCO

*Warren Alpert Medical School of Brown University; Center for Clinical Cancer Informatics and Data Science (CCIDS)*

---

## ABSTRACT

Wikipedia is among the most accessed health information sources and depends entirely upon volunteer editors. Medical trainees have contributed meaningful edits through WikiMed electives and edit-a-thons but have lacked data-driven tools to identify articles with the greatest reach and the greatest need for improvement. We developed an open-source web application featuring a dedicated Cancer Dashboard. Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) descriptors were assigned to WikiProject Medicine-flagged articles through a custom pipeline, and cancer-specific articles identified by filtering those descriptors against MeSH subtrees. Each article was scored using a normalized Impact-Need Score (0-100) combining pageview reach, editorial importance labels, quality deficit, editor scarcity, and clickstream-derived search intent. Among 1,859 cancer-related articles, the median Impact-Need Score was 63.7 (IQR 55.1-71.9), 57.5% were rated Stub or Start quality, and the median Flesch-Kincaid reading grade was 13.6 (IQR 11.6-15.7). The dashboard is available at wikimed-dashboard.com under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, with all code and pipelines open-source and auto-updated monthly.


## INTRODUCTION

Wikipedia is among the most consulted health information resources in the world. Its medical content drew an estimated 6.5 billion page views in 2013 across all language editions, exceeding the traffic of the NIH, WebMD, Mayo Clinic, NHS, WHO, and UpToDate on comparable measures [1]. It is equally prominent at the point of search, appearing among the first ten results for 71-85% of health queries tested across major search engines, ahead of MedlinePlus and NHS Direct Online, and ranking highest of all for rare diseases [2]. A scoping review across patient, student, and clinician populations found it consistently prominent as a health information resource [3].

That content is written entirely by volunteers, and its quality is uneven. Audits have repeatedly found Wikipedia's medical articles substantively accurate but difficult to read. A comparison of ten cancer topics against the National Cancer Institute's Physician Data Query database found errors rare in both sources, but Wikipedia written at Flesch-Kincaid grade 14.1 against PDQ's 9.6 [4]. Comparable audits exist for drug information [5] and for the growth of medical content over time [6].

WikiProject Medicine (WPM) volunteers assign each article two ratings: editorial quality (Stub to Featured Article) and clinical importance (Low to Top) [7]. These labels provide a rough signal of need, but browsing category trees does not scale across 53,286 articles, and the articles with the largest gap between readership and quality are not visible from category pages.

Prior work has therefore characterised Wikipedia's medical content retrospectively and within bounded disease areas. What has been missing is a prospective, corpus-wide tool that ranks articles by where editorial effort would reach the most readers. We developed a customized algorithm and dashboard to address this unmet need, with a dedicated Cancer Dashboard as the first domain-specific application.


## METHODS

**Article metadata.** Five sequential Wikipedia and Wikimedia API phases supplied quality class, importance label, watcher count, article length, 12-month pageviews, and unique editor counts for all 53,286 WPM articles.

**Supplemental signals.** Separate pipelines added inbound link counts, 3-month pageview totals for traffic velocity, and search-intent fractions derived from the May 2026 Wikimedia clickstream dataset. TF-IDF scores computed from article lead sections were matched against the 2026 NLM MeSH vocabulary to produce a Medical Relevance Score (1-10).

**MeSH assignment.** A seven-layer cascade assigned MeSH descriptors, ordered by data reliability rather than coverage, with match confidence retained as metadata.

**Cancer identification.** Assigned descriptors were filtered against the MeSH C04 (Neoplasms) subtree and against antineoplastic agent descriptors reached through MeSH PharmacologicalAction links, which capture drugs classified by chemical structure rather than clinical use. A title-keyword fallback covered articles without a MeSH assignment.

**Scoring.** A normalized Impact-Need Score (0-100) combined pageview reach (30%), importance (25%), quality deficit (25%), editor scarcity (10%), and search intent (10%). All components were percentile-ranked within the full corpus before weighting. Readability was estimated by Flesch-Kincaid (FK) grade level from article lead sections.

**Dashboard.** Built in Python (Streamlit, Plotly, pandas) and deployed on Streamlit Community Cloud, with a monthly GitHub Actions workflow re-fetching signals and recomputing all scores automatically. [Figure 1]


## RESULTS

**Corpus and identification.** Of 53,286 WPM articles, 1,859 were identified as cancer-related: 1,266 (68%) through the MeSH C04 subtree, 168 (9%) through antineoplastic agent descriptors, and 425 (23%) through the title-keyword fallback. MeSH coverage reached 89% across the cancer subset. [Figure 2]

**Editorial need.** Median Impact-Need Score was 63.7 (IQR 55.1-71.9). 57.5% of cancer articles were rated Stub or Start quality, compared with 67.7% corpus-wide; 17 of 1,859 (0.9%) had reached Good or Featured Article status. [Figure 3]

**Where the gap sits.** Clinical importance and editorial quality are already partly aligned. Of the 65 articles rated High or Top importance, 43 (66%) were B class or better and only 4 were Stub or Start. The editorial gap instead sits in the long tail: 1,060 Stub or Start articles rated Low or Mid importance, which together drew 5.67 million views over 12 months, 14% of all cancer article readership, at a median of 1,365 views each. [Figure 4]

**Readability.** Median FK grade level was 13.6 (IQR 11.6-15.7). 68.0% of cancer articles were written above grade 12, and 1.4% at or below the eighth-grade level of the average US adult. [Figure 5]


## DISCUSSION

The Impact-Need Score surfaces the intersection of public reach and editorial gap, not the worst-quality articles alone. The cancer results show why that distinction matters. WikiProject Medicine volunteers have already brought most high-importance cancer articles to B class or better, so a prioritization strategy resting on importance labels alone would direct student effort toward the articles needing the least work. The articles with the largest remaining gap are individually rated Low or Mid importance but collectively reach millions of readers each year, a pattern visible only when readership is measured directly rather than inferred from editorial labels.

Readability is a parallel and largely independent gap. A cancer article at grade 13.6 sits above the level most adults read comfortably regardless of its quality class, and raising an article from Stub to C does not by itself make it more accessible.

**Limitations.** Quality and importance ratings are volunteer-assigned and may lag actual article content. The MeSH C04 subtree includes benign neoplasms, so the corpus is neoplasm-related rather than strictly malignant. Readability is computed from lead sections rather than full articles. The scoring weights are theoretically motivated and have not yet been validated against editing outcomes.


## CONCLUSIONS

* Identified 1,859 cancer-related articles from 53,286 WikiProject Medicine articles using the MeSH C04 subtree, antineoplastic PharmacologicalAction links, and a keyword fallback, achieving 89% MeSH coverage across the subset

* Derived a normalized Impact-Need Score combining readership, clinical importance, quality deficit, editor scarcity, and clickstream search intent

* Found the editorial gap concentrated not in high-importance articles, 66% of which are already B class or better, but in a 1,060-article long tail drawing 5.67 million annual views

* Measured a median reading grade of 13.6, with 68% of cancer articles written above grade 12 and 1.4% readable by the average US adult

* Deployed a public dashboard with automated monthly refresh at wikimed-dashboard.com, licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, with all code and pipelines open-source on GitHub

* A pre-post survey study across seven partner medical schools to gauge utility, usability, and usefulness is planned

## Decisions made, and why

- **MeSH coverage is reported as 89% (cancer subset), not 82% (corpus).** The submitted abstract states 82% corpus-wide coverage. The data does not support that figure: actual corpus coverage is 60.9% (32,430 / 53,286), and has been since the initial commit. The 43,700 figure on the WIN poster is exactly 82% x 53,286, derived from the percentage rather than counted. Cancer-subset coverage of 89% is accurate, higher, and more relevant to this poster, so it is used here. The dashboard Methodology page also states ~82% and needs the same correction.
- **The priority-matrix finding differs from the WIN poster framing.** The WIN poster describes top-left cells (high importance x low quality) as the biggest unmet need. For the cancer subset that is not what the data shows: only 4 of 65 High/Top importance articles are Stub or Start. Presenting the long-tail finding instead is both accurate and a stronger informatics result, since it is only visible by measuring readership directly.
- **Biographies remain in the cancer corpus.** Henrietta Lacks (802,455 views) and Joseph Merrick (598,243) are among the ten most-read articles in the cancer subset, and Belatacept, a transplant immunosuppressant, ranks second by Impact-Need. The MeSH pipeline matches titles against descriptor names, so surnames collide with eponymous syndromes. A detector exists (fetch_is_biography.py) but has only been run against 147 precision-oncology articles. Running it corpus-wide before the meeting would clean the live dashboard behind the poster's URL.
- **Figure palettes were validated for colour-vision deficiency.** The dashboard's six-class quality ramp fails adjacent-pair separation under normal vision on two pairs (Start/C = 12.8, GA/FA = 13.5, against a floor of 15), and six near-blues are unreadable at poster viewing distance. Quality is collapsed to three validated classes in Figure 4 (worst-case CVD separation 18.7). GA and FA together account for only 17 of 1,859 articles, so nothing material is lost.
